Data Governance

April 6, 2026 · 2 min

Data Governance is the framework of policies, roles, and processes that define who can do what with data, and how that data is managed across its entire lifecycle.

It answers three fundamental questions:

  • Who owns the data?
  • Who is allowed to access it?
  • How do we ensure it stays accurate, consistent, and compliant?

Core Components#

1. Data Ownership#

Every dataset has a designated Data Owner — typically a business stakeholder (not IT) — who is accountable for its accuracy and proper use. Alongside them, a Data Steward handles the day-to-day quality checks and enforcement.

2. Data Catalog#

A searchable inventory of all data assets in an organization. It answers: “What data do we have, where does it live, and what does it mean?”

Examples: Alation, Microsoft Purview, Apache Atlas, dbt docs

3. Data Lineage#

Tracks the full journey of a piece of data — from its source system, through every transformation, to its final destination in a dashboard or model. Critical for debugging and compliance audits.

4. Access Control#

Defines who can read, write, or delete specific datasets. Usually enforced through role-based access control (RBAC) at the warehouse or lake level.

5. Data Quality Rules#

Formalized rules that data must pass before it’s considered trustworthy. Examples: no null primary keys, valid date ranges, referential integrity between tables.

Why It Matters#

Without governance, organizations end up with:

  • Multiple versions of the same metric with different numbers (“Which revenue figure is correct?”)
  • Compliance violations (GDPR, HIPAA) from uncontrolled data access
  • Silent data corruption that propagates into reports and ML models
  • No accountability when data quality issues occur

Governance vs. Data Management#

Data Management Data Governance
Focus Technical: how data is stored and processed Policy: rules, roles, and accountability
Who does it Data Engineers Data Owners, Stewards, Compliance teams
Output Pipelines, schemas, warehouses Policies, catalogs, access controls

They are complementary — governance sets the rules, management enforces them technically.
